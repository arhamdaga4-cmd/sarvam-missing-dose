Voice check-in agent · Sarvam + Exotel

A daily call that asks one question that matters.

Patients stop taking medicines quietly — nobody notices for weeks. Missing Dose phones them at home, asks in their own language whether today's doses were taken, listens for symptoms, reminds them about the next appointment, and hands anything worrying to a human coordinator the same morning.

The call

Four topics, then a clean goodbye

The dialogue is not open-ended chatter. A state machine walks the same four topics every time, so a check-in can be compared across days, and the extraction step can ask for exactly the fields it needs.

Medication adherence

Did today's doses go in? If not, how many were missed and why.

Side effects

Any discomfort after taking them — one short answer, then move on.

Symptoms

Pain, dizziness, breathlessness, swelling. One clarifying question if it sounds serious.

Appointment

The next visit date, and whether they can actually get there.

Structured report

A second, separate model call turns the transcript into strict JSON — with null where the patient never answered.

Why extraction is a separate call

A chatty model will happily skip a missed dose while being warm about it. Keeping the extraction prompt separate, with its own instructions and temperature zero, means the report can be validated on its own terms — and when it fails, it says parse_error instead of inventing a number.

Architecture

What sits between the phone line and the models

Exotel carries the call and streams raw audio over a WebSocket. The bridge in the middle owns turn-taking: it buffers until it hears silence, transcribes, runs one dialogue turn, speaks the reply back, and clears the speaker the moment the patient interrupts.

Audio bridge

telephony/bridge.py

Framework-free state machine: RMS turn detection over 20 ms frames, barge-in via clear, 30-second utterance cap, and idempotent finalisation.

04

Sarvam models

sarvam_client.py

Three endpoints, one subscription key. Speech in, dialogue, speech out.

05

Report & escalation

escalation.py · notifier.py

Rule-based triage into urgent / soon / info, then delivery to console, JSONL, webhook or email — recording which channels actually went out.

06

Storage & scheduler

patient_db.py · scheduler.py

SQLite keeps patients, check-ins, transcripts and escalations, so adherence history survives a restart. The scheduler wakes daily, skips anyone already called, and retries a failed attempt after three hours.

Triage

Escalation is a rule set, not a vibe

A coordinator has to be able to argue with the logic that woke them up, so the thresholds are plain rules in one file — and levels never downgrade once raised.

Level What triggers it What happens
Urgent Chest pain, breathlessness, fainting or bleeding — matched in any supported language — or any symptom the model graded severe. Coordinator alert with the patient's own words quoted. A human calls back the same day.
Soon Two or more missed doses, "didn't take them today", a declined appointment, an unparseable report, or three days with no completed check-in. Follow-up within a day, by webhook or email.
Info One missed dose, side effects mentioned, transport help needed. Logged for the next review. Nobody is paged.
None Everything answered, nothing flagged. Check-in stored. No notification.

Before you point it at a patient

Make one test call on your own number, keep the API behind a private network, and treat the SQLite file as medical data — it holds transcripts in plain text. Consent and an opt-out path are not built yet.

Known limits

What this does not do yet

Written down here so nobody discovers them on a live call.

Turn-based, not streaming. Each utterance costs one transcription, one dialogue turn and one synthesis request — roughly two to four seconds of silence between the patient finishing and the agent replying.

The voice activity detector is an RMS gate. It will cut people off in a noisy room or on speakerphone. Tune the threshold, or swap in a real VAD.

No authentication on the HTTP API. Patient names and transcripts are readable by anyone who can reach the port.

One language per patient, fixed when the call starts. Code-mixed speech is transcribed fine, but the dialogue stays in a single language.

No consent capture and no opt-out. A real deployment needs both, plus a retention policy for the transcripts.
